### Abstract
Password-based authentication remains the foundational access control mechanism across modern computing systems despite decades of known vulnerabilities [TODO: Citation needed - Authentication surveys, e.g., Bonneau et al., 2012]. Users consistently balance security requirements against cognitive retention limitations, frequently producing passwords susceptible to automated dictionary attacks, credential stuffing, and offline hash recovery [TODO: Citation needed - Password usability & breach studies, e.g., Ur et al., 2015]. 

This project presents **PasswordGuard**, an educational, client-side web application designed to evaluate password strength, illustrate combinatorial search complexity, and demonstrate the mechanics of cryptographic hashing and salting. The application incorporates a transparent heuristic scoring algorithm (0–100), theoretical character-space entropy estimation under a uniform-character assumption, and illustrative brute-force search time projections under varied attacker capability profiles. Crucially, the system operates under a strict privacy-by-design model, executing entirely within local browser memory without persistent storage or network transmission. 

To evaluate the functional consistency of the heuristic analyzer, an experiment was conducted on a controlled dataset of 150 synthetic passwords divided into five distinct structural categories. The findings show how the heuristic responds across categories: multi-word passphrases achieved an average theoretical character-space entropy estimate of 182.9 bits with 21 of 30 samples (70.0%) classified as Strong despite utilizing only two character sets, whereas superficially complex short passwords incorporating digits and symbols collapsed under sequential and common pattern penalties (averaging 10.5 out of 100, with 29 of 30 samples classified as Weak).
